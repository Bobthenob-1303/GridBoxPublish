Printing Press

The printing press, developed by Johannes Gutenberg in Europe around the 1440s–1450s, used movable metal type. Individual letters could be arranged into a page, inked, and pressed onto paper repeatedly.

Redundancy

A scribe might spend months or years producing a single book. The printing press allowed the same page of type to produce hundreds of copies. The Henry Ford notes that printing became progressively faster and cheaper as presses improved, allowing printed material to be distributed on a mass scale
